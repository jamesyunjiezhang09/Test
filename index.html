SHEET 02 / 06

Knee Fatigue Wearable

A wearable device that estimates real-time fatigue in the muscles surrounding the knee — built for submission to IEEE SSCS.

IEEE SSCS

Sensor Brace + Arduino Backend

Sensing: FSR load, motion intensity, skin temp, rotation
Output: live fatigue score, 0–100
Platform: Arduino + BLE companion app

Force-sensitive resistors and IMU modules are housed in 3D-printed red mounts stitched into a compression knee brace, streaming to a companion app that turns raw sensor load into a single, legible fatigue reading. Field-tested under real load — deadlifts in the living room, sprints and kicks on the pitch — to validate the model beyond the bench.

"Active · Peak 17 · 100% FSR Load" — live readout from the companion app
SHEET 03 / 06

Posture Correction Device

A wearable pendant that senses leaning posture against the gravity vector and nudges the wearer back upright — filed as a patent, and named finalist at the provincial Youth Innovation Fair.

PATENT FILED

Necklace Housing + Haptic Feedback

Result: Finalist, Youth Innovation Fair
Core parts: IMU, BLE, haptic actuator, coin cell
Form: decorative pendant, swappable cover

An inertial sensor tracks angular displacement of the chest against gravity; past a threshold, a haptic actuator taps a gentle reminder to sit up. The electronics live inside a coin-sized PCB, sealed behind a laser-cut decorative cover so the device reads as jewelry rather than a gadget.

SHEET 04 / 06

Cognitive Load & Center of Mass

An instrumented chair study on how cognitive-load activities shift a seated person's center of mass — honourable mention at GVRSF.

GVRSF · HONOURABLE MENTION

Load-Cell Instrumented Chair

Sensors: 4× load cell, one per chair leg
Logging: microcontroller + SD card
Question: does mental workload shift posture?

A load cell under each leg of an ordinary chair logs weight distribution while subjects complete tasks of varying cognitive demand — quantifying subtle shifts in center of mass that track with mental effort, not just physical fidgeting.
